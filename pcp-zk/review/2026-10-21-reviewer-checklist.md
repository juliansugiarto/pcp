# Reviewer checklist for the bit-level SHA-256 chip

*Written 21 October 2026 before the review of the new chip (inside the project, by a separate AI reviewer that did not write the code), and used by that review (`2026-10-21-newchip-review.md`). Items 7 and 12 describe an earlier draft of the chip; see the review's finding L-3. Item 16: the tool named there (Picus) has no halo2 front end; the halo2 analyser is Korrekt (quantstamp/halo2-analyzer), which was not run.*


### 1.1 What I verified by reading, so the reviewer can start from here

- **Seed ordering.** Rows 0..3 of the compression region hold `A[-4..-1] = IV3, IV2, IV1, IV0` and `E[-4..-1] = IV7, IV6, IV5, IV4`, so at round 0 `Rotation(-1)` is `a = IV0`, `Rotation(-4)` is `d = IV3`, and likewise `e = IV4`, `h = IV7`. Correct. The seeds are pinned by `assign_advice_from_constant` into `aval`/`eval`, and the `q_init` gate ties the bit columns to those packed cells with booleanity. The C-class "free IV" bug is closed.
- **Round algebra.** `s0 = Σ0(A[t-1])` from `abit` at `-1` with `rotr(2,13,22)`; `s1 = Σ1(E[t-1])` with `rotr(6,11,25)`; `Ch(E[t-1],E[t-2],E[t-3])`; `Maj(A[t-1],A[t-2],A[t-3])`; `E[t] = d + h + Σ1 + Ch + K_t + W_t - ecar·2^32` with `d = aval(-4)`, `h = eval(-4)`; `A[t] = h + Σ1 + Ch + K_t + W_t + Σ0 + Maj - acar·2^32`. Matches FIPS 180-4. Carries: 6 addends → `ecar ∈ 0..5`, 7 addends → `acar ∈ 0..6`, enforced by `range_poly` which is the exact product `∏(x−i)`. The C-2 class is closed.
- **`K_t` and IV** are in a fixed column (`kcol`), read by `query_fixed`. C-1 closed.
- **Schedule.** `W_0..7` are the caller's key cells copied into `aval` (copy constraint) with booleanity and packing by `q_sched_word`, so the chip itself proves each key word is 32 bits; `W_8..15` are `assign_advice_from_constant` of `PADDING = [0x80000000, 0×6, 256]`, the correct one-block padding for 32 bytes; `W_16..63` are gate outputs with `σ0(W[i−15])`, `σ1(W[i−2])`, `W[i−16]`, `W[i−7]`, carry `0..3`. SHR zero-fill is handled by dropping the term when `j+n ≥ 32`. C-3 closed, because `W_t` enters each round by `copy_advice` of the schedule's constrained cell.
- **Digest.** Operands are `a_cells[67..64] = A[63..60]` and `e_cells[67..64]`, copied into `wc`; `IV[m]` fixed; out bits boolean and packed; carry boolean. C-4 closed.
- **Rotation conventions.** `bits_of` is LSB-first and `pow2(i)` packs LSB-first; `rotr(n, j) = (j+n) mod 32` gives bit `j` of `x >>> n`. Consistent. Word-to-byte order of `H` is handled outside the chip and was reviewed OK earlier.
- **Test hooks** are `cfg(test)` and touch only witness and fixed *values*, never `configure`. Good.

### 1.2 What the reviewer must still check (the classic failure modes, made concrete for this chip)

1. **Every referenced cell is assigned on every row where its gate's selector is on.** The round gate references 64 bit cells at each of `cur, -1, -2, -3` plus `aval/eval` at `-4`; the schedule gate references `abit` at `-2` and `-15` and `aval` at `-7` and `-16`. List each gate's rotations and confirm the region assigns every one of those cells on every selected row. In halo2 0.3.5 an unassigned cell under a live selector is a MockProver error; under a dead selector it is silently fine, which is why the next item matters.
2. **Selector coverage count.** Expected: `q_init` on exactly 4 rows, `q_round` on exactly 64, `q_sched_word` on 16, `q_sched` on 48, `q_digest` on 8. Write a test that walks the assigned selectors and asserts those counts. Table16's gaps were, in part, constraints that existed on paper and were not enabled on the rows that mattered.
3. **Negative rotations stay inside the region.** The three regions (schedule, compression, digest) share the `abit`, `aval`, `s0`, `s1`, `acar` columns. `SimpleFloorPlanner` places regions one after another in call order; a gate at a region's first selected row must only look back at rows that the same `assign_region` closure assigned (round 0 at row 4 looks at seed rows 0..3; schedule row 16 looks at rows 0..15). Confirm no selected row reaches back past its region's row 0. Also confirm nothing relies on wrap-around: `Rotation(-1)` at domain row 0 is the last row of the domain, which is a blinding row.
4. **Booleanity on every free bit column, on every row where the bit is used.** Bits that are *outputs* of gate equations (`s0`, `s1`, `ch`, `maj` are packed sums, not bit columns) need no check; bits that are *witnessed* (`abit`, `ebit` on seed, round, schedule and digest rows) do. The gates check `abit` and `ebit` on round rows, `abit` on schedule and digest rows, both on seed rows. Verify there is no row where a bit column is read by a gate at a rotation without having been boolean-checked by the gate that owns that row (e.g. the round gate reads `ebit` at `-3`; row `t−3` is a round or seed row, both of which check `ebit`). Write this as a table: column × row-kind × which gate checks booleanity.
5. **Packing identity on the same row as the bits.** `aval = Σ abit·2^i` must be enforced by a gate *enabled on that row*. It is, on all four row kinds; confirm no row has a packed value used by a later rotation (`aval` at `-4`, `-7`, `-16`) whose own row lacks the packing gate.
6. **Carry ranges are exact and the field cannot help.** With all addends < 2^32 and the packed result forced < 2^32 by its bit decomposition, `sum = out + carry·2^32` is an integer identity as long as `carry` is small, and `range_poly` forces it. Confirm the `hi` arguments: 5, 6, 3 and the boolean carry in the digest. A `hi` one too large is harmless; one too small breaks completeness, not soundness; a missing range check is C-2.
7. **The fixed column is identical at keygen and proving.** `kcol` is assigned inside `synthesize`, unconditionally, including when witnesses are `Value::unknown()`. Confirm the `tamper_fixed` hook can never fire outside `cfg(test)`, and see item 11 about what a fixed-column tamper test does and does not show.
8. **No witness-dependent control flow touches selectors or fixed cells.** Grep `synthesize` for any branch on a `Value` that changes which selector is enabled or which fixed value is written. The verifier recomputes the VK from `SaleCircuit::default()`; any such branch is either a VK mismatch (loud) or an exploit (silent).
9. **Equality columns.** `constrain_equal`/`copy_advice` require `enable_equality` on both columns; halo2 panics at keygen otherwise, so this is loud. Still check that the *only* cross-region links are the intended ones: 8 key cells → schedule rows 0..7; 64 `W_t` → round rows; 8 state cells → digest rows; 8 digest cells → instance. Anything else is a smell.
10. **Instance endianness and layout.** `H` words big-endian from bytes, instance rows in the documented order, bits LSB-first inside a word. One known-answer test with a 32-byte key against the SPEC vector 2 (`H = 02d449a3…`) proves completeness only; also keep the negative test that a byte-swapped `H` fails.
11. **The adversarial suite must test what it claims.** A `TAG_K` tamper that changes the fixed column changes the *circuit*; MockProver with a consistent circuit and a consistent trace will pass or fail for reasons unrelated to soundness. Fixed cells cannot be chosen by the prover; the proof that `K_t` is bound is the column type, not a test. Keep the tamper tests to witness cells: one bit non-boolean on each row kind, one `WordDelta` on `A`, `E`, `W_i` and digest, one `CarryDelta` on each carry column, one swapped `W_t` copy. Each must fail against the honest VK. The global `poc_hook` state is thread-local; run with `--test-threads=1` and `clear()` in every test.
12. **Dead cells.** The four "placeholder" assignments into `chc`/`majc` at row 0 of the compression region write the same cell four times. Harmless (no selector reads them there), but remove them; a reviewer should not have to prove that a strange assignment is harmless.
13. **Degree and the proof-system limits.** `xor3` is degree 3, `maj` 3, `ch` 2, `range_poly(·,6)` is degree 7, all times a selector. halo2 reports the max degree at keygen; it will be lower than Table16's 9. Nothing to check by hand except that no constraint exceeds the configured limit, which is loud.
14. **Column count versus memory.** The chip adds 64 bit columns plus about 14 more: roughly 78 advice columns against 10 before. In halo2 0.3 every advice column is a polynomial in the extended domain, so at `k = 17` this is several gigabytes and will exceed the 6.27 GB cgroup limit before proving finishes. But the only reason `k` was 17 is Table16's 2^16-row spread table, which is gone. Re-tune `k` *before* measuring: the SHA part is 140 rows, the sudoku and Poseidon parts fit in a few thousand, the nibble table is 16 rows. **M** that `k` drops to 13 or below and both time and memory fall well under the Table16 numbers; measure and put it in `COSTS.md`.
15. **The CLI (H-2).** `verify` must take the buyer's own puzzle, the invoice's `H` and a required binding, and take only `C` from the seller. This is spec rule V4 restated; the previous CLI violated it. Test: a seller-supplied `public.json` with different givens must fail.
16. **Tooling, optional.** Under-constrained-circuit detectors for halo2 exist (Veridise's Picus has a halo2 front end; others) **[U: current availability and whether they handle 0.3.5]**. Worth an afternoon if one runs; not a substitute for items 1–15.

The method for all of this is one sentence: **every advice cell has exactly one owner**, a gate on its own row or a copy constraint from an owned cell, and the reviewer's artefact is the table that says which.
